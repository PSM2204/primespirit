How to Use This Predictor

Before you dive into any number on this page, understand one thing clearly: there are two completely separate systems filling government MBBS seats. They run simultaneously and you should be registered for both.

The 15% All India Quota (AIQ) is handled by MCC at mcc.nic.in. It is open to students from every state in India. The competition here is national, which means the cut-offs are higher. The 85% State Quota is handled by each state's own counselling authority and is generally restricted to domicile holders of that state. The cut-offs here are lower, the competition is within one state, and this is where the majority of government MBBS seats actually get filled.

When you look at the closing rank for, say, Patna Medical College under state quota in Bihar — that closing rank is for Bihar domicile holders only. A student from Maharashtra cannot compete for that seat. This is why your state matters enormously in how you read these numbers.

Category-wise Strategy: What Your Reservation Actually Gets You

Reservation in NEET counselling is one of the most misunderstood aspects of the process. Let us go through each category clearly.

OBC-NCL Students

Under AIQ, the OBC and General closing ranks in 2025 were almost identical (AIR 26,178 vs 26,231). This surprises most students. The real OBC advantage is in state quota counselling, where states like Maharashtra (52% OBC reservation), Karnataka (32%), and UP (27%) provide significantly more accessible closing ranks for OBC candidates. Make sure your OBC-NCL certificate is from the current financial year before counselling begins.

SC Students

SC candidates have strong advantages in both AIQ and state quota. In AIQ 2025, SC seats closed at AIR 1,36,445 — meaning SC students with scores as low as around 395 marks still secured government MBBS through AIQ. Under state quota, SC closing ranks extend even further. The key is ensuring your certificate is from a recognised authority.

ST Students

ST candidates had the most accessible cut-offs in 2025 under AIQ, with the last seat closing at AIR 1,62,975 — approximately 360 marks. State quota ST closing ranks are lower than this in most states. However, the number of ST category seats in top colleges is small, so the actual competition for premium ST seats (AIIMS, MAMC, VMMC) remains significant.

EWS Students

EWS closes marginally better than General under AIQ. In 2025 the difference was roughly 2,000 to 6,000 ranks. The EWS certificate must be specifically issued for central government admission purposes by a tehsildar or SDM. A general income certificate is not acceptable.

How to Build Your Choice Filling Strategy Using These Numbers

Knowing the closing ranks is only half the work. What you do with them during choice filling is what actually determines which college you get. Here is the approach we use at Prime Spirit Mentors when guiding students through counselling.

Step 1: Establish your realistic range

Take your AIR and category. Find which tier of colleges you fall in from the tables above. Mark the colleges where your rank falls comfortably within the closing rank (not at the edge of it). These are your safe choices.

Step 2: Build an ambitious tier above

Look at colleges where you are 2,000 to 5,000 ranks above the 2025 closing rank. These are your stretch choices — possible if 2026 sees a slight relaxation in that college's competition. Always fill these above your safe choices. There is no downside to putting AIIMS Delhi at the top even if you are rank 20,000. If it is available, you get it. If not, the algorithm moves down.

Step 3: Include low-competition state colleges as your floor

If your state has accessible government MBBS options (Bihar, Jharkhand, Odisha for example) and you have domicile, include these in your state quota choice list. They are the safety net that ensures you do not end up with nothing even if your ambitious choices do not materialise.

Step 4: Do not skip private colleges in AIQ

Deemed university MBBS through MCC has higher fees but provides security. For students near the AIQ edge (AIR 20,000–30,000 range), having a few deemed university choices at the bottom of your AIQ list ensures a seat even if all government options close above your rank.

Step 5: Lock — do not just fill

This is the most common reason students lose everything: they fill choices but forget to click Lock before the deadline. Unlocked choices are not processed. Always lock, take a screenshot of the locked list, and set a reminder for the closing window.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q. What is the minimum NEET 2026 score for a government MBBS seat?
For General category students through AIQ, you need roughly 615–640 marks to be in a safe range. Under state quota in less competitive states like Bihar, Jharkhand, and Odisha, General category students with scores around 500–530 marks also secured government MBBS seats in 2025. The answer genuinely depends on your category, your home state, and which quota you are targeting.
Q. Which state has the lowest NEET cut-off for government MBBS?
Bihar, Jharkhand, and Odisha consistently have the most accessible state quota closing ranks. In 2025, General category students with AIR 40,000–60,000 secured government MBBS seats in these states through state quota counselling. For SC and ST category students, the closing ranks extend even further.
Q. Can I get AIIMS with 650 marks in NEET 2026?
AIIMS Delhi is not accessible at 650 marks — it closed at rank 47–57 in 2025 which requires 700+ marks. However, 650 marks would put you in range for several newer AIIMS campuses like AIIMS Jodhpur, Bhopal, Rishikesh, and Bhubaneswar, where General category closing ranks in 2025 were in the AIR 1,500–4,000 range. Fill all AIIMS campuses in your choice list and let the algorithm work.
Q. Will NEET 2026 cut-off be higher or lower than 2025?
Experts expect NEET 2026 cut-offs to be slightly higher than 2025 because the re-exam conducted on June 21, 2026 was reported as moderate difficulty. In 2025, the paper was tough, which pushed qualifying cut-offs down to 144 marks. In 2026, qualifying cut-offs are expected around 142–150 marks for General, and admission cut-offs for government MBBS under AIQ are expected in the 615–640 range.
Q. Is 550 marks enough for government MBBS in 2026?
At 550 marks, Government MBBS through AIQ for General category is unlikely. However, state quota counselling in Bihar, Jharkhand, Odisha, and Chhattisgarh is genuinely possible if you are a domicile holder of those states. OBC, SC, and ST candidates at 550 marks have better options including state quota seats in moderately competitive states. It is worth registering for both MCC and your state counselling and participating fully before making any alternative decision.
Q. Are these closing ranks official or estimated?
The closing ranks on this page for NEET 2025 are based on MCC Round 3 (final round) official data and verified state counselling trends. For NEET 2026, no official closing ranks exist yet — those will only be published after MCC counselling, which is expected in August 2026. The 2026 figures mentioned are projections based on difficulty analysis and past year trends. Always verify at mcc.nic.in before taking any admission decision.
Q. Do I need to register separately for AIQ and state quota?
Yes. These are entirely separate registrations. AIQ counselling requires registration at mcc.nic.in with its own fee. State quota requires registration at your state's counselling portal. Both processes run simultaneously. Missing the state registration window because you were waiting for AIQ results is one of the most common and costly mistakes students make. Register for both as soon as the windows open.
Q. Why are AIQ OBC closing ranks almost the same as General?
Under AIQ, OBC reservation is 27% of seats. However, the pool of OBC candidates competing nationally is also very large. In 2025, AIQ closing ranks for General and OBC-NCL were nearly identical (AIR 26,178 vs 26,231). The real OBC benefit appears in state quota counselling where reservation percentages are often higher (Maharashtra is 52%, for example) and the competition pool is smaller.
Q. My home state is Bihar but I want a college in Karnataka. Can I apply?
You can apply for Karnataka's government colleges through AIQ (15% seats) without a Karnataka domicile. But for Karnataka's 85% state quota seats, you need a Karnataka domicile. As a Bihar domicile holder, you participate in Bihar's state quota for the 85% seats there and compete nationally for AIQ seats everywhere. You cannot claim state quota in any state other than your home state.
Q. What if I do not get a government MBBS seat in any round?
If government MBBS does not materialise through any round, your options include: private MBBS through MCC deemed counselling; BDS in a government college (much more accessible); BAMS/BHMS in government Ayush colleges through AACCC; or preparing for NEET 2027. The decision between taking a private MBBS seat and preparing again is deeply personal and financial — it deserves careful thought rather than a panic decision made on the day results are announced.
