Why BHU Still Matters for Science Students in 2026

A central university campus setting. Banaras Hindu University's 1,300-acre campus is one of the largest residential universities in Asia. | Source: Unsplash

Established in 1916 by the visionary scholar Pandit Madan Mohan Malaviya, Banaras Hindu University is not merely a college — it is an academic ecosystem. Ranked 11th overall by NIRF in 2023 and consistently among the top central universities in India, BHU sits at a unique intersection: the cultural and spiritual gravity of Varanasi, the academic prestige of a central university, and fees that are among the lowest for any premier institution in the country.

For science students from PCM, PCB, or combined streams, BHU offers something genuinely rare — a wide catalogue of rigorous undergraduate science programmes (BSc, BTech through IIT BHU, MBBS through IMS-BHU, B.Pharm, BSc Agriculture) at a fraction of the cost of private universities, on a self-contained campus with world-class research infrastructure.

But getting in requires more than a good CUET score. The counselling process itself has tripped up thousands of capable students over the years. This guide exists so that does not happen to you.

📌 Quick Clarification: BHU vs IIT BHU

These are two legally distinct institutions that share the same campus in Varanasi. BHU (Banaras Hindu University) admits students through CUET UG for BSc, BA, BCom, and related programmes. IIT BHU (Indian Institute of Technology, BHU) admits students through JEE Advanced for BTech and related engineering programmes. A student cannot apply to both using the same exam. This article covers BHU (CUET-based admission) in detail, with a section on IIT BHU for context.

How BHU Admission Actually Works in 2025–26

Until 2021, BHU conducted its own University Entrance Test (UET) — a separate exam that lakhs of students prepared for specifically. That changed. From 2022 onwards, all UG admissions at BHU are based exclusively on CUET UG scores conducted by the National Testing Agency (NTA). There is no BHU-specific entrance exam anymore.

Here is how the sequence works:

1

Appear in CUET UG (conducted by NTA)

Register at cuet.samarth.ac.in. Choose subjects matching your Class 12 stream. The exam is held between May and June each year. You must select BHU as a participating university during CUET registration.

2

CUET Results Declared

NTA releases CUET UG scores, usually in July. Your performance in the relevant subjects determines your eligibility score for BHU.

3

BHU Counselling Registration

BHU opens its counselling portal at bhuonline.in. Register using your CUET application number. Pay the non-refundable counselling registration fee (typically ₹300–₹500) via debit card, credit card, or net banking.

4

Fill Preferences (Most Critical Step)

Log into the portal and fill your course preferences in priority order. This is where most mistakes happen. Do not leave any blank. Arrange from most to least preferred. Mistakes here cannot be undone after locking.

5

Seat Allotment Round 1

Based on your CUET score, merit list position, category, and preferences, BHU allots a seat. Check the portal. If satisfied, pay the seat confirmation fee and secure it.

6

Upgradation Rounds (Round 2–5)

If you want a better course or did not get your first preference, participate in subsequent rounds. BHU typically conducts 3 to 6 rounds including a spot round. You may upgrade but your current seat remains until you accept a new allotment.

7

Document Verification & Physical Reporting

Once you lock your seat, report to the university with original documents for verification and complete the formal admission process within the stipulated date.

⚠️ Important: No Call Letters in 2026

As per updates for the 2026–27 session, BHU has announced that no physical call letters will be issued. Everything is managed online through bhuonline.in. All communication happens via SMS and email registered at the time of CUET. Ensure your contact details are accurate and your inbox is not ignoring BHU notifications as spam.

Science Courses at BHU: Who Can Apply for What

BHU's Institute of Science houses some of the best-equipped research laboratories among central universities in India. | Source: Unsplash

BHU does not have a single "science faculty." Science programmes are distributed across multiple institutes — the Institute of Science (for core BSc subjects), the Faculty of Agriculture, the Institute of Medical Sciences (IMS-BHU for MBBS and BDS), and the Faculty of Ayurveda (for BAMS). Understanding which institute runs which programme helps you fill preferences correctly.

💡 Did You Know?

BSc (Hons.) Computer Science at BHU costs just ₹16,000 for the entire 4-year programme in tuition fees — that is less than what many private colleges charge for a single semester. Yet it admits students with CUET scores in the 400–500 range, and graduates are recruited by top IT firms including IBM, Infosys, and Cognizant.

Important: Chemistry Has Two Groups

One detail that confuses many students: BHU offers BSc (Hons.) Chemistry in two distinct groups — the Maths Group and the Biology Group. These are listed separately in the preference form. PCM students should choose Chemistry (Maths Group). PCB students should choose Chemistry (Biology Group). Filling the wrong one can result in rejection even if your score is sufficient.

MBBS and Medical Courses: A Different Route

MBBS at the Institute of Medical Sciences (IMS-BHU) does not use CUET. Admission is entirely through NEET UG scores via MCC counselling (Medical Counselling Committee). The 15% All India Quota seats are managed by MCC. The remaining 85% are state quota seats distributed through UP NEET counselling. IMS-BHU offers 100 MBBS seats on a 1,200-bed hospital campus — making clinical exposure substantial right from the early years of the programme.

🏥 IMS-BHU: What Makes It Special

The MBBS programme at IMS-BHU spans 5.5 years including a one-year mandatory internship. Students get extensive clinical exposure at the Sir Sunderlal Hospital, a 1,200-bed facility. The programme includes pre-clinical subjects (Anatomy, Physiology, Biochemistry), para-clinical subjects (Pathology, Pharmacology, Microbiology), and clinical rotations in Medicine, Surgery, Obstetrics, Paediatrics, and more. For PCB students with NEET ranks between 1,500 and 3,500 (General category, AIQ), IMS-BHU is a realistic and prestigious target.

CUET Cutoff Trends 2023–2025 for BHU Science Courses

The most important thing to understand about BHU's CUET cutoffs: they have been rising consistently year on year. The competition for the 2025 admissions cycle was noticeably steeper than 2024, which was already steeper than 2023. Plan your CUET preparation with this trajectory in mind.

The overall CUET cutoff range for General category UG admissions at BHU shifted from approximately 8–540 in 2023 to 225–517 in 2024, and then to 229–652 in 2025. That upward pressure at the top end reflects growing awareness of BHU among CUET-appearing students.

9 Costly Mistakes Students Make in BHU Counselling

Counselling stress is real, but most BHU admission mistakes are avoidable with proper preparation. | Source: Unsplash

These are not hypothetical warnings. They come from documented patterns — students who lost their preferred course to someone with a lower CUET score simply because of procedural errors. Each one of these is more common than you might think.

Mistake 01

Not Checking the Portal Daily

BHU releases round dates, cutoffs, and seat allotments with sometimes very short notice windows — sometimes just 24–48 hours for response. Students who check the portal once every few days miss critical deadlines and lose their allotted seat.

Mistake 02

Filling Preferences Without Research

Many students fill the preference form on the day it opens, without looking at previous year cutoffs. They place dream courses that need 600+ marks near the bottom of their list — below courses they care far less about — and end up with a seat they never wanted.

Mistake 03

Leaving Preferences Blank

The BHU system treats a blank preference as disinterest. If you leave sections empty, the algorithm skips those courses entirely — even if they were your backup plan and even if seats are available. Fill every eligible option.

Mistake 04

Confusing BSc Chemistry (Maths vs Bio Group)

This is BHU-specific. Chemistry for PCM students and Chemistry for PCB students are listed as separate programmes. Choosing the wrong group makes you ineligible even if your overall CUET score is excellent. Read the eligibility column carefully.

Mistake 05

Wrong CUET Subject Selection

Some students appear in CUET without selecting "Banaras Hindu University" as a participating institution, or they choose wrong CUET subject combinations for BHU's requirements. Check BHU's official course-specific CUET subject requirements before your CUET registration.

Mistake 06

Ignoring Later Counselling Rounds

Many students who do not get their first-preference course in Round 1 assume the process is over. It is not. BHU typically runs 3–6 rounds. Seats open up as students who got multiple offers elsewhere withdraw. Students who remain active in the portal in later rounds often end up with excellent courses.

Mistake 07

Failing to Pay the Seat Confirmation Fee

Getting a seat allotment does not mean you have the seat. You must pay the seat confirmation fee within the stipulated window — sometimes just 24 hours. Missing this means your allotment is cancelled and the seat goes to the next candidate on the merit list.

Mistake 08

Document Mismatch

Students arrive for document verification with photocopies that do not match the originals, or with documents that are not self-attested as required. Common culprits: category certificates that are not current, income certificates for EWS that have expired, or Aadhaar details that do not match Class 12 records.

Mistake 09

Applying Only Through BHU, Not Elsewhere

BHU should rarely be your only counselling destination. Smart students simultaneously participate in CUET-based counselling at Delhi University, JNU, Allahabad University, and AMU. BHU counselling opens and closes quickly — having parallel applications protects you if BHU does not work out.

✅ The 48-Hour Rule for BHU Counselling

Treat every step of BHU counselling with a 48-hour mental deadline. If a deadline is 3 days away, complete the action in the next 48 hours. Portal glitches, net banking failures, and last-minute connectivity issues are real. Building this buffer can be the difference between securing a seat and losing it.

BHU Counselling Timeline: What Happens When

February–April

CUET Registration

Register for CUET UG at cuet.samarth.ac.in. Select BHU as a participating university. Choose your subject combination carefully based on the BHU courses you intend to apply for.

May–June

CUET Exam Conducted

The CUET UG exam typically runs in computer-based mode across multiple cities. For 2025, the exam was held between mid-May and late May. Results usually come in July.

July

CUET Results & BHU Counselling Opens

NTA declares results. BHU activates its counselling portal (bhuonline.in) shortly after. Counselling registration typically opens in the first week of July and runs for approximately 7–10 days.

Late July

Preference Filling & Locking

The most important phase. You have approximately 5–7 days to study cutoffs, discuss with mentors, and fill your preference form. Once locked, it cannot be edited for that round.

August

Round 1 Seat Allotment & Confirmation

BHU releases the Round 1 allotment list. Check your portal. Pay the seat confirmation fee within the given window. If unhappy, participate in Round 2 without withdrawing your current seat.

August–September

Rounds 2–5 / Spot Round

Subsequent rounds are held as seats vacate. The final spot round (if conducted) typically offers remaining seats on a first-come, first-served basis to eligible candidates physically present at BHU.

September–October

Document Verification & Classes Begin

Report to BHU with originals. The academic session typically begins in late August or September. Hostel allotment happens separately after physical reporting.

Myth vs. Fact: What Students and Parents Believe (But Shouldn't)

🔴 Myth: "A 600+ CUET score guarantees admission to any BHU science course."

Fact: A 600+ score is competitive but not a guarantee. Courses like BSc Agriculture (629 cutoff, 2025) and BSc Medical Radiology (628 cutoff) already have closing scores near or above 600. The cutoff is a closing score — not an entry ticket. Candidates above the cutoff get seats based on total merit list rank and availability. Also, the subjects you appeared for in CUET must match the course eligibility.

🔴 Myth: "BHU is only good for arts students. Science students should aim for IITs."

Fact: This is a widespread misconception. BHU's Institute of Science has produced CSIR fellows, ISRO scientists, IAS officers, and leading medical researchers. For students who do not make it to IITs or AIIMS, BHU's BSc programmes — particularly Physics, Mathematics, and Computer Science — offer outstanding research access at minimal cost. Several BHU BSc graduates have gone on to PhD programmes at IITs, MIT, and Stanford.

🔴 Myth: "BHU counselling is like JoSAA — if you don't take the seat, you're out."

Fact: BHU's system allows you to participate in multiple rounds without losing your existing seat until you choose to upgrade or withdraw. If Round 2 gives you a better course, you accept it and the Round 1 seat is released. If Round 2 does not improve your allotment, you retain your Round 1 seat. This is different from some other counselling systems where failing to participate in a round means losing your seat.

🔴 Myth: "BSc from BHU has no value in the job market."

Fact: This conflates the general challenge of a BSc in India with BHU specifically. The NIRF 2026 data shows a median placement package of ₹8.5 LPA for BHU's 3-year UG batch — a figure that exceeds the average salary at many engineering colleges. Moreover, BSc graduates who qualify CSIR-NET, IIT JAM, GATE, or UPSC get placement outcomes that exceed ₹12–20 LPA in research, civil services, or teaching roles.

For Parents: Understanding What Your Child Is Choosing

If you are a parent reading this alongside or instead of your child, this section is for you.

BHU operates as a residential university. Your child, if admitted, will live on a 1,300-acre self-contained campus in Varanasi. Hostel accommodation is available and regulated. The environment is disciplined, culturally rich, and academically rigorous — but it is also far from most Indian cities in terms of its pace and atmosphere.

The single most valuable thing you can do is not impose a course choice based on perceived prestige. Many parents push their PCB children toward BSc Medical Radiology because it sounds clinical, without considering that the child's aptitude and interest might be better suited to BSc Zoology or BSc Botany, which have stronger research outcomes. CUET scores are finite. Placement form filling should prioritise your child's genuine interests.

👨‍👩‍👧 Parent Tip: Visit the Campus if Possible

If you can visit BHU's campus before the counselling period, do so. Spending even one day at the university — talking to current students, walking through the science departments, seeing the hostels — gives you more useful information than most online guides. BHU's open culture generally welcomes prospective families. The campus is accessible by train to Varanasi Junction (Kashi station is even closer).

CUET Preparation Tips Specific to BHU Admission

🧠 Quick Quiz: Test Yourself

Q: A PCB student with a CUET score of 580 applies only to BSc Agriculture (cutoff 629) at BHU and locks no other preferences. What happens?

A: They do not get any BHU seat — because their score is below the cutoff for their only preference. If they had also filled BSc Zoology (cutoff ~600), BSc Botany, or BSc Biochemistry as backup preferences, they might have secured a seat. Never gamble on a single preference.

Subject Strategy for PCM Students Targeting BHU

CUET scoring for BHU considers your performance in the subjects relevant to your chosen programme. For BSc Mathematics, your Mathematics/Applied Mathematics score in CUET carries maximum weight. For BSc Physics, Physics + Mathematics scores matter. Make sure to appear in the General Test as well — BHU may factor it in for some courses.

Subject Strategy for PCB Students Targeting BHU

For Biology-stream courses at BHU (Zoology, Botany, Biochemistry, Agriculture), your Biology score in CUET is the primary determinant. For BSc Agriculture, if you have appeared in Agriculture as a CUET subject, that score directly applies. PCB students without Agriculture in CUET can still apply to BSc Agriculture using Biology scores — check the latest BHU course information sheet for the exact subject mapping each year, as it has been updated between sessions.

The 1,300+ acre campus functions as a self-contained township with a hospital (Sir Sunderlal Hospital), bank branches, post office, shopping areas, sports facilities including a swimming pool and stadium, and multiple places of worship.
BHU is particularly strong in Physics, Chemistry, Mathematics, and Agricultural Sciences for research. The university houses DST-FIST supported departments, the Interdisciplinary School of Scientific Instrumentation, and multiple DBT-funded projects. BSc students who approach faculty proactively in the 2nd year often get opportunities to work in labs, publish papers, and build a research portfolio that is competitive for IIT JAM and international PhD applications.
ABC stands for Agriculture, Biology, and Chemistry as the Class 12 subject combination — common in vocational or agricultural science streams. Students with this combination are eligible to apply for BSc (Hons.) Agriculture, BSc (Hons.) Horticulture, BSc (Hons.) Forestry, BSc (Hons.) Food Technology, and similar agriculture-allied programmes at BHU through CUET. They are generally not eligible for core physics or mathematics-based BSc programmes.
