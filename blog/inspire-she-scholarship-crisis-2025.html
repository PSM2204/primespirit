If you or your child is pursuing undergraduate or postgraduate science at an IISER, IISc, NIT, or any other institution on the strength of their top-1% Class 12 board rank or JEE/NEET performance, you likely know about the INSPIRE-SHE scholarship. It is — or was — the government's most direct statement that choosing pure science over engineering or medicine was financially viable.

In 2025, that statement went missing.

"My family is financially weak and I have been waiting for almost a year. We have received no information. The government should either continue the scholarship or at least explain why it has been stopped."

What Is INSPIRE-SHE?

Launched in 2008, the Innovation in Science Pursuit for Inspired Research (INSPIRE) programme is one of India's most comprehensive government efforts to attract young talent toward scientific careers. Its Scholarship for Higher Education (SHE) component sits at the heart of this: it awards ₹60,000 per year as a monthly stipend (₹5,000/month) plus ₹20,000 as an annual mentorship grant, totalling ₹80,000 per academic year, for up to five years.

Eligible students fall into two broad categories: those who rank among the top 1% in their Class 12 board examinations, and those admitted to premier institutions via national entrance exams like JEE, NEET, JBNSTS, or who have won Science Olympiads. The scholarship is disbursed directly into SBI savings accounts via Direct Benefit Transfer (DBT).

For context: ₹80,000/year nearly covers a student's annual mess expenses at many IISERs. For students from economically weaker backgrounds, it is not a supplement — it is a lifeline.

A Timeline of Silence

Every September, 2009–2024

DST releases the annual INSPIRE-SHE notification. Applications open, close by October-November, and the final merit list is published by May–June the following year.

September 2024

No notification appears for the 2025 batch. Students and institutions wait, assuming a delay.

January 2025

The Vigyan Dhara scheme comes into effect, merging INSPIRE with two other DST umbrella schemes. DST describes it as a "strategic integration" to enhance efficiency.

March 18, 2026

Pranit Sohane, a BSc student from Hamirpur, UP, files an RTI application asking DST about the 2025 notification's status.

April 15, 2026

DST replies to the RTI: "This is to inform you that the INSPIRE SHE-2025 advertisement will not be announced." No reason given.

June 16, 2026

DST separately informs IIT Kanpur that the INSPIRE SHE-2025 advertisement will not be announced, and that any student lists submitted by institutions cannot be considered.

July 15, 2026

Hindustan Times publishes an investigation. DST and Ministry of Science & Technology have still not publicly responded to any queries about discontinuation, delay, or future cycles.

The Vigyan Dhara Merger: Efficiency or Erasure?

In January 2025, the Union Cabinet approved "Vigyan Dhara" — an umbrella scheme merging three DST programmes: the S&T Institutional and Human Capacity Building scheme (under which INSPIRE-SHE was funded), Research and Development, and Innovation, Technology Development and Deployment.

The government described it as a move to "enhance efficiency in fund utilisation" and "establish synchronization among sub-schemes." The ministry called Vigyan Dhara "a catalyst for India's scientific progress" and "a strategic integration."

The numbers tell a different story. While the government cited a budget increase from ₹330.75 crore to ₹1,425 crore for Vigyan Dhara in 2025-26 (later revised to ₹2,009.14 crore before being brought back down to ₹1,425 crore in 2026-27), a closer look at the constituent schemes reveals the INSPIRE-SHE component of the budget was cut nearly 40% at the Revised Estimates stage — from ₹543.91 crore to ₹542.38 crore, against a Budget Estimate of ₹900 crore. The scheme received no separate budgetary allocation in the 2025-26 and 2026-27 Union Budgets.

Important context

The INSPIRE-SHE scholarship has also merged with broader policy shifts. The Kishore Vaigyanik Protsahan Yojana (KVPY) was discontinued in 2022. The National Talent Search Examination (NTSE) has been under an extended "review" since 2021. With INSPIRE-SHE now silent, there is no central government scholarship left for undergraduate science students who choose basic sciences.

Who Gets Hurt — And How

The impact is being felt unevenly. Students already receiving INSPIRE-SHE (prior batches) are mostly continuing to get their money — though with reported delays and administrative chaos caused by the Vigyan Dhara restructuring. The crisis falls hardest on new entrants: students who joined undergraduate science programmes in 2025 and qualified for the scholarship but never received a notification to apply.

"I enrolled in a BSc programme relying on INSPIRE-SHE and had not applied for other scholarships because I expected support under the scheme. I missed other merit-based scholarships because of uncertainty. It is becoming financially difficult for me to continue my studies."

— Pranit Sohane, BSc student, Hamirpur (UP)

"My family is financially weak and I have been waiting for almost a year. Because of our financial constraints, I have had to postpone buying a laptop or tablet, which is essential for coursework, coding and assignments."

— Rishav Bhuin, second-year BS-MS student, IISER Pune

"My family is financially weak and I have been waiting for almost a year. We have received no information. The government should either continue the scholarship or at least explain why it has been stopped."

— Dhrubajyoti Mahata, IISER Tirupati student

The dual-scholarship restriction compounds this pain directly: because students cannot hold two government fellowships simultaneously, many chose not to apply for alternative scholarships — confident that INSPIRE-SHE would come. It did not.

What Educators and Experts Are Saying

The academic community is not staying quiet. Prof. Anindita Bhadra of IISER Kolkata described the decision as "another disaster for higher education," warning that if scholarships stop completely, only well-off families will be able to send their children to institutions like the IISERs.

Prof. V. Ramgopal Rao, former Director of IIT Delhi and Vice-Chancellor of BITS Pilani, called programmes like KVPY and INSPIRE among India's most effective mechanisms for identifying and nurturing scientific talent, adding: "If India wants to become a global leader in science and technology, we cannot weaken the very pipeline that produces our future scientists. Investing in young scientific talent is one of the best investments any nation can make."

Student organisations including the ABVP have issued statements calling the situation "a matter of serious concern" and urging DST to take "swift corrective measures."

What Should Affected Students Do Right Now?

  • Do not wait passively. The RTI route has already produced the clearest answer the government has given: the 2025 notification will not come. Plan your finances accordingly for this academic year.
  • Apply for alternative scholarships immediately. The National Scholarship Portal (NSP) has several merit-based and category-specific scholarships. Do not let the INSPIRE uncertainty prevent you from securing other funding — the dual-scholarship bar only applies if you are actually awarded INSPIRE.
  • Check your institution's internal scholarships. IISc, for example, is providing ₹5,000/month to all undergraduates regardless of INSPIRE status. Several other institutions have similar provisions. Speak to your academic administration.
  • File RTI applications collectively. If your student body can coordinate a batch of RTI applications asking about future cycles and budgetary allocations, it creates a paper trail and increases pressure for an official response.
  • Keep academic performance high. If INSPIRE-SHE resumes — even under the Vigyan Dhara umbrella — continuation criteria (minimum 60% marks or GPA ≥ 7.0) will remain. Falling behind now closes doors later.
  • Monitor the official INSPIRE portal. The portal at online-inspire.gov.in is where future announcements, if any, will appear first. Bookmark it and check regularly.

A note for parents

If your child has chosen a BSc/BS-MS programme in basic sciences on the understanding that INSPIRE-SHE would provide financial support, have an honest conversation now. The scholarship may resume — but it may not resume for the 2025 batch. Do not let financial uncertainty push a capable science student toward a career change. Explore institutional scholarships, state government awards, and private foundations as bridges.

The Bigger Picture for Indian Science

India's gross expenditure on R&D as a percentage of GDP hovers around 0.64% — compared to 2.4% for China, 3.5% for the United States, and 5.7% for Israel. Brain drain from India's science ecosystem is not new, but it accelerates when the financial floor for young scientists disappears.

The INSPIRE-SHE scholarship was not just money. It was a signal: that the Indian government valued the choice to study pure science, that a student in a BSc programme at a college in a smaller city could receive the same recognition as one at an IIT. Removing that signal — especially without communication — sends a very different message.

The Vigyan Dhara scheme can theoretically restore and even expand INSPIRE-SHE's reach. The cabinet-approved total outlay for 2021-26 is ₹10,579.84 crore. Whether student-facing scholarships are prioritised within that envelope is a policy choice, not a financial impossibility.

Until the government speaks clearly — on whether the 2025 cycle is permanently cancelled, deferred, or being restructured — thousands of India's brightest science students are being asked to make life decisions in the dark.

"Investing in young scientific talent is one of the best investments any nation can make." — Prof. V. Ramgopal Rao, Former Director, IIT Delhi

A Note for Bengaluru Science Students

Students in Bengaluru — across Koramangala, HSR Layout, Whitefield, Jayanagar, Indiranagar, and beyond — are among the highest-density INSPIRE-SHE recipients in India. Many students enrolled in BSc and BS-MS programmes at institutions like IISc, IISER, and affiliated science colleges in Karnataka qualified for this scholarship based on their CBSE/ICSE board performance or JEE/NEET ranks.

With the 2025 notification absent, Bengaluru families who made financial plans around INSPIRE-SHE need to act on alternatives quickly. Karnataka State Government scholarships (through the Karnataka Scholarship Portal), PM-YASASVI, and institution-specific grants are all active. If your child is currently in Class 11 or 12 preparing for JEE, NEET, IAT, or NEST, do not let the scholarship uncertainty become a reason to pause preparation — if anything, a strong rank opens up scholarship doors that are entirely independent of INSPIRE.
