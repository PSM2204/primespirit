Spot Round Admission 2026: What Actually Happens to DU, BHU and ICAR Agriculture Seats After the Regular Rounds End

Every July, a particular kind of panic shows up in agriculture-aspirant WhatsApp groups. A student has decent CUET marks, has watched two or three allotment rounds go by, and still doesn't have a confirmed seat anywhere. Someone mentions "spot round" like it's a secret door. It isn't a secret. It's a documented, rule-bound stage of counselling — but it behaves very differently from the rounds before it, and that difference is exactly what trips students up.

This chapter walks through three systems that often get mentioned in the same breath but run on separate tracks: Delhi University's own CSAS spot admission, BHU's Central Admission Committee spot round for its Institute of Agricultural Sciences, and the ICAR AIEEA counselling pipeline that feeds seats into 75-odd agricultural universities across the country. Knowing which one applies to you — and knowing it before the portal opens, not after — is most of the battle.

Quick Summary

A spot round is not a repeat of the regular round with lower cutoffs. It only opens after regular allocation is exhausted, it fills only what remains vacant, and in most systems it drops the preference-based algorithm in favour of direct, first-eligible selection. Whether you can even enter one depends on rules specific to DU, BHU, or ICAR — they are not interchangeable.

1. What a "spot round" actually is

Regular counselling rounds work on a matching algorithm. You rank your preferences, the university ranks its applicants by merit, and a seat gets allotted where both lists intersect. After two or three such rounds, some seats are still empty — not because no one wanted them, but because of a chain reaction: a student allotted Seat A upgrades to Seat B in round two, vacating Seat A; someone allotted Seat C never pays the fee and forfeits it; a category-reserved seat has no eligible claimant in that cycle. All of this residue becomes the vacant-seat pool.

The spot round exists purely to clear that pool before the academic session starts. That single fact explains almost every rule that follows: no fresh preference list to rank, usually no reshuffling once you accept, and a compressed timeline because the university needs classes to begin.

Students assume a spot round means "easier cutoff, same as before." In reality, it means a fixed, shrinking list of specific seats — you are choosing from what's left, not being ranked against a fresh applicant pool with the same odds as round one.

2. DU spot admission for agriculture-adjacent seats

Delhi University itself does not run a BSc Agriculture programme — agriculture aspirants land on DU pages mostly while searching allied science, biology, or agriculture-economics-adjacent CUET-based courses, or because they are simultaneously tracking DU for a sibling course while applying to BHU or an ICAR institute for agriculture proper. It still matters to understand DU's process because the sequence — CSAS registration, CUET score, allocation rounds, upgrade window, then spot admission — is the template most central-university systems in India now follow.

Once the University of Delhi completes its regular CSAS UG allocation-cum-admission rounds, any seats that remain unfilled are opened through one or more spot admission rounds, where DU publishes a college-wise and programme-wise vacant-seat list on the CSAS portal. Unlike the regular rounds, where an algorithm allots seats by preference and merit, spot admission works closer to first-eligible: you choose from what is actually vacant rather than from a ranked list.

To be eligible, you must already be registered on CSAS UG using your CUET application number, currently hold no DU seat (or have withdrawn from one), and meet the minimum eligibility and CUET-based cutoff for the specific programme in the spot round. You log in with your existing CUET credentials — there is no fresh registration if you already registered for CSAS UG — select the spot admission option, and pick from colleges and programmes showing vacancies that match your eligibility.

Once you accept a seat in a DU spot round, you cannot upgrade to a "better" vacancy in a later spot round, and withdrawal is either unavailable or heavily restricted — missing the acceptance deadline cancels the offer instantly with no reminder round. Treat your first eligible seat as close to final.

3. BHU spot round and the Institute of Agricultural Sciences

This is the one most directly relevant to agriculture aspirants, because BHU's Institute of Agricultural Sciences (Faculty of Agriculture, Varanasi campus, plus the Rajiv Gandhi South Campus at Mirzapur) runs both undergraduate agriculture programmes and a large postgraduate cluster across Agronomy, Soil Science, Genetics and Plant Breeding, Entomology, and related departments.

For 2026, BHU's Central Admission Committee (CAC-BHU) opened online registration for its PG spot round from 10 to 14 July, with a ₹500 fee for new candidates and allotment declared on 16 July. The mechanism sits on top of an admission that is otherwise anchored to CUET-PG scores: admission to BHU's MSc Agriculture programme is based on CUET-PG scores, with over 200 seats spread across 11 specialised departments, a semester fee of roughly ₹12,500 for regular seats, and ₹42,500 per semester for paid supernumerary seats.

On the undergraduate side, the pathway runs through the national CUET-UG rather than a separate BHU entrance test. BHU BSc Agriculture admission is based on CUET-UG, and for ICAR supernumerary seats the same CUET-UG Agriculture-domain score is used — ICAR no longer conducts a separate AIEEA-UG exam of its own; it now relies on CUET-UG scores for its affiliated institutions. This is a genuinely important shift for anyone still preparing based on older information: the standalone AIEEA-UG paper for ICAR-affiliated UG seats has effectively been folded into CUET-UG.

BSc Agriculture fees differ noticeably by campus — the main Varanasi campus (FMC) charges a total programme fee of about ₹80,000 across four years for regular seats, while the Rajiv Gandhi South Campus at Mirzapur charges ₹30,000 per semester under its special-fee structure; SC/ST candidates with family income under ₹1 lakh a year get a full tuition fee waiver. ICAR supernumerary seats at the main campus follow the same fee structure as FMC's regular seats.

If your Class 12 board subject was Agriculture rather than the standard PCB or PCM combination, that is not a disadvantage — it is an edge. The CUET Agriculture paper (Domain Code 401) covers the Class 11–12 Agriculture syllabus including Agronomy, Soil Science, Horticulture, Animal Husbandry, Plant Physiology, and Agricultural Economics, and students who actually studied that syllabus in school have a clear advantage over PCB/PCM candidates attempting it cold.

What to carry if BHU's spot round allots you a seat

Candidates allotted a seat in BHU's PG spot round need to carry their Provisional Admission Letter, BHU Registration Form, CUET/NTA documents (application form, admit card, and scorecard), all UG, Intermediate and High School mark sheets and certificates, category certificate where applicable, transfer and migration certificates, character certificate, anti-ragging undertaking, a residence/dossier form, and six passport-size photographs — originals along with one photocopy set, for verification at the time of reporting. Prepare this folder before results are even out; you will not have time to hunt for a migration certificate once allotment is announced.

4. ICAR AIEEA and the agriculture counselling ladder

ICAR sits one level above individual universities — it is the coordinating body whose entrance scores and counselling process feed seats into dozens of institutions at once, BHU among them. Understanding its structure clears up why "ICAR spot round" and "BHU spot round" are not the same question, even though a BHU agriculture seat can be reached through either door.

The ICAR AIEEA-PG 2026 exam was held on 4 July 2026, in computer-based mode conducted by NTA, with 120 MCQ questions worth 4 marks each and a negative mark of 1 for wrong answers, to be completed in two hours; application forms were open from 8 May to 10 June (extended), with fees of ₹1,300 for General category and ₹675 for SC/ST/PwBD/Third Gender candidates. It serves admission to Master's programmes across Agriculture, Horticulture, Forestry, Veterinary and Animal Sciences, Agricultural Engineering, Community Science, Fisheries, and Dairy Science, spanning 64 State Agricultural Universities, 4 ICAR-Deemed Universities (IARI, IVRI, NDRI, CIFE), 3 Central Agricultural Universities, and 4 Central Universities with Agriculture faculties — BHU, AMU, Visva-Bharati, and Nagaland University.

For UG seats, the process now runs through the national CUET-UG rather than a separate ICAR paper: the counselling sequence includes online registration, choice filling, seat allotment, online seat acceptance, document verification at the allotted university, final admission, and finally a spot round to fill any seats still vacant after the regular rounds. NTA does not set a single fixed qualifying mark; each participating university sets its own cutoff based on seats, applicant numbers, and category-wise reservation, though a score above roughly 700 out of 1000 is generally considered strong for top ICAR-linked universities such as IARI, NDRI, PAU, and BHU. After the AIEEA-UG result, ICAR conducts online counselling at icarcounseling.com, tentatively from the first week of September, to fill about 15% of UG seats reserved across 74 ICAR-accredited Agricultural Universities.

The counselling process itself follows a familiar shape: registering on the ICAR website with the counselling fee (usually around ₹400), downloading a counselling letter containing the schedule, and then logging into the counselling portal to fill preferred choices of programme and university.

Parents often ask whether a child who "only" gets an ICAR spot-round seat at a lesser-known State Agricultural University is settling for less. 75 institutions accept ICAR AIEEA/CUET-UG scores, and while IARI and NDRI are essentially PG/PhD-focused, well-established SAUs such as PAU, GBPUAT, and TNAU are genuine top destinations for the BSc programme — the ICAR umbrella itself is a quality filter, not a fallback.

6. Documents and mistakes that cost a seat

The single most common failure in every spot round is arriving without a document that was never asked for in earlier rounds — typically the migration certificate, the anti-ragging undertaking, or enough passport photographs. Spot rounds compress verification into hours, not days.
  • Keep your CUET application form, admit card, and scorecard as both physical printouts and scanned PDFs.
  • Category certificates (SC/ST/OBC-NCL/EWS/PwBD) must be current — an expired income-and-asset certificate for EWS is a frequent rejection reason.
  • Six passport photographs is a specific, recurring requirement in BHU's process; carry more than you think you need.
  • If you are applying across DU, BHU, and an ICAR-affiliated SAU simultaneously, maintain separate folders — the document sets overlap but are not identical.
If you get a seat offer in a spot round while still waiting on a preferred option elsewhere, ask yourself honestly: is the wait based on a real cutoff trend, or on hope? A confirmed seat in hand is worth more than a marginally better one that may never materialise.

7. A working strategy for the last week

  1. Track the official portal, not forwarded WhatsApp screenshots. Vacancy lists and dates shift by a day or two routinely; only admission.uod.ac.in, the BHU CAC portal, and icarcounseling.com are authoritative.
  2. Decide your floor before results appear. Write down, in advance, the lowest-ranked programme-college combination you would actually accept. Decisions made under a ticking countdown are rarely your best ones.
  3. Don't discount smaller SAUs. A recognised State Agricultural University seat filled through the ICAR umbrella is a completed, ICAR-accredited degree — not a compromise credential.
  4. If nothing clears anywhere this year, that is not the end of the option. CUET and AIEEA are annual; a focused second attempt with a clearer strategy is a legitimate path, not a failure.
"Choice filling is not guessing — it is ranking real vacancies against your actual, current eligibility." — a line worth writing on the inside cover of your counselling file.

Key Takeaways

  • Spot rounds fill only what regular rounds left vacant — they are not a second regular round with easier cutoffs.
  • DU has no standalone BSc Agriculture, but its CSAS spot-admission process is the structural template most central universities follow.
  • BHU's Institute of Agricultural Sciences takes both UG (via CUET-UG) and PG (via CUET-PG) students, with its own PG spot round.
  • ICAR no longer runs a separate AIEEA-UG exam — CUET-UG now feeds ICAR's UG supernumerary seats; AIEEA-PG remains a distinct exam for PG admission.
  • Document readiness, not last-minute luck, decides who actually converts a spot-round offer into a confirmed seat.

8. Frequently asked questions

What exactly is a "spot round" in university admissions?

It is the final counselling stage, run only after all regular allocation rounds are complete, that offers candidates whatever seats remain vacant — through direct selection from a published list rather than a fresh preference-ranking algorithm.

Does Delhi University offer a BSc Agriculture programme?

No. DU does not run a standalone undergraduate Agriculture degree; agriculture aspirants generally look to BHU or ICAR-affiliated State Agricultural Universities instead.

Is the ICAR AIEEA-UG exam still conducted separately?

No — ICAR no longer conducts its own AIEEA-UG exam; ICAR supernumerary UG seats now use the CUET-UG Agriculture-domain score instead.

Is AIEEA-PG still a separate exam from CUET-PG?

Yes, for 2026 the AIEEA-PG remained a distinct national-level computer-based exam conducted by NTA on ICAR's behalf, separate from the CUET-PG process some universities use for their own postgraduate programmes.

How many institutions accept ICAR AIEEA / CUET-UG agriculture scores?

75 institutions in total — 64 State Agricultural, Horticultural, Veterinary and Fisheries Universities, 3 Central Agricultural Universities, 4 ICAR-Deemed Universities, and 4 Central Universities with Agriculture faculties, including BHU.

What documents does BHU require at spot-round reporting?

Provisional Admission Letter, BHU Registration Form, CUET/NTA application form, admit card and scorecard, all UG/Intermediate/High School mark sheets and certificates, category certificate where applicable, transfer and migration certificates, character certificate, anti-ragging undertaking, residence/dossier form, and six passport photographs, in original plus one photocopy set.

Can I upgrade my seat after accepting a DU spot-round offer?

Generally no — once accepted, upgrading to a later, better vacancy is not available, and withdrawal is heavily restricted or not offered at all.

What is a competitive AIEEA-UG / CUET-UG Agriculture score for top ICAR institutions?

A score of roughly 700 or above out of a 1000-mark aggregate across four subjects is generally considered very competitive for top ICAR-linked universities such as IARI, NDRI, PAU, and BHU — though there is no single official cutoff, since each participating university sets its own.

Do SC/ST students get fee concessions for BHU BSc Agriculture?

Yes — SC/ST candidates whose family income is below ₹1 lakh per year receive a 100% tuition fee waiver at BHU's main campus.

How much does BHU's MSc Agriculture programme cost?

Regular seats cost about ₹12,500 per semester (roughly ₹50,000 across the two-year programme), while paid supernumerary seats cost ₹42,500 per semester.

What is the eligibility for BHU MSc Agriculture admission?

A four-year BSc Agriculture or BSc (Hons.) Agriculture/Horticulture degree from an ICAR-recognised university, with a minimum OGPA of 6.0 out of 10 for General/OBC/EWS candidates and 5.5 out of 10 for SC/ST/PwBD candidates.

Does studying Agriculture in Class 12 help in CUET?

Yes — the CUET Agriculture domain paper directly covers the Class 11–12 Agriculture syllabus, giving students who studied that subject in school a real edge over PCB or PCM candidates attempting it without that background.

How many total ICAR seats are offered nationally?

Roughly 15,000 graduate and 11,000 postgraduate seats are offered across the ICAR-participating universities each year.

Is spot admission open to students who never registered for the regular counselling rounds?

This depends on the specific university's current-year bulletin — some systems allow fresh registration at the spot stage while others, like DU's process in most recent cycles, require you to have already registered through the regular round. Always check the exact wording of that year's official notification rather than assuming last year's rule still applies.

What happens to a seat if I miss the spot-round fee payment deadline?

The offer is cancelled immediately in most systems, with no reminder or grace round — treat the payment deadline as non-negotiable and complete it well before the final hour.

Where can I check official ICAR counselling schedules?

The authoritative source is icarcounseling.com along with icar.org.in; treat aggregator websites as useful summaries but always cross-verify dates against these official portals before acting.
