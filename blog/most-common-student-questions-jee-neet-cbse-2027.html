Most Common Student Questions: JEE Mains, NEET & CBSE Boards (2027)

By Abhinav Kashyap · Prime Spirit Mentors · Updated September 2026

Every week, parents and students ask us some version of the same handful of questions — on WhatsApp, during demo calls, in comment sections. Instead of answering them one at a time, here they are, answered straight, with no sales pitch buried inside.

On Study Hours & Balancing Boards

How many hours should I study daily for NEET or JEE Mains?

There's no universal number, and anyone who gives you one (6 hours! 10 hours!) is guessing. What actually matters is focused hours, not hours logged. 5-6 hours of distraction-free, active studying — solving problems, not re-reading notes — outperforms 10 hours with your phone next to you. Most Class 11-12 students balancing boards manage 6-8 focused hours on holidays and 3-4 on school days. Build from there instead of chasing a number.

Can I prepare for NEET or JEE while also scoring 90%+ in boards?

Yes — and for NEET specifically, boards and competitive prep overlap heavily since both lean on NCERT. Physics, Chemistry and Biology conceptual depth for NEET is close to board depth. The gap is bigger for JEE Maths and Physics numericals, which need dedicated practice beyond what boards test. The efficient approach: master NCERT to board level first (Layer 1), then layer on JEE numerical speed or NEET conceptual MCQs (Layer 2/3) — not two separate, disconnected preparations.

On Drop Years & Repeat Attempts

I'm a dropper. Should I repeat the whole syllabus or only revise weak topics?

Re-teaching yourself the entire syllabus from zero is one of the most common drop-year mistakes — it feels productive but wastes months on chapters you already know. A focused strategy built on error mapping from your previous attempt — pinpointing exactly which topics and error types cost you marks — paired with heavy mock test volume, produces far better rank improvement in the same 10-12 months.

On Exam Anxiety & Mental Performance

I know the material, so why do I still blank out or panic during the actual exam?

This is almost never a knowledge gap — it's a mental performance issue, and it needs a different fix than "study more." Timed mock simulations under real exam conditions, a deliberate question-selection triage strategy (deciding what to attempt first, what to skip), and structured psychometric support for identifying your specific anxiety triggers address this directly. Piling on more content usually makes the anxiety worse, not better.

On Batch Size & Coaching Choice

Does a smaller batch size actually make a measurable difference?

Yes, mechanically. In a 5-student batch, a mentor can watch each student's problem-solving in real time, catch a repeated calculation error the same session instead of it compounding for weeks, and adapt pacing per student. In a 200-student batch, this is structurally impossible — even the best faculty in the world can't personally track 200 individual error patterns. It's not about faculty quality; it's about what batch size physically allows.

The Short Version

Study smart hours, not maximum hours. Board prep and NEET prep overlap more than people assume. A drop year should fix specific errors, not repeat the whole syllabus. Exam anxiety needs its own strategy, separate from content revision. And batch size isn't a marketing detail — it determines whether a mentor can actually see you.
