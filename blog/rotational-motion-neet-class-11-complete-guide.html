Rotational Motion for NEET 2026: Complete Guide, PYQ Breakdown & 2-Week Revision Plan

4 to 8 marks. Every single year. Rotational Motion is one of the most predictable chapters in NEET Physics — yet most students either over-prepare it or skip the wrong sub-topics. This guide tells you exactly what to study, what to skip, and how to not leave easy marks on the table.

Why Rotational Motion matters more than you think

NEET Physics has 45 questions worth 180 marks. Rotational Motion (officially called System of Particles and Rotational Motion in NCERT Class 11) contributes 1 to 2 questions every year — that is 4 to 8 marks. In a competitive exam where the difference between a government MBBS seat and no seat can be a single question, these are marks you cannot afford to miss.

NEET 2026 Syllabus Update
The NTA has added Basic Concept of Rotational Motion (angular velocity ω, angular acceleration α) as a new topic for 2026. Three earlier topics have been removed: Momentum Conservation, Centre of Mass Motion, and Centre of Mass of Uniform Rod. Make sure your notes reflect this change if you are using older material.

Topic-by-topic breakdown

1. Moment of Inertia (I)

The rotational equivalent of mass. It tells you how difficult it is to change the rotation of an object. NEET almost always asks you to calculate I for a standard shape about a specific axis — then apply the Parallel Axis Theorem or Perpendicular Axis Theorem to shift the axis.

Standard results to memorise: Thin rod (centre): I = ML²/12
Thin rod (end): I = ML²/3
Ring (centre): I = MR²
Disc (centre): I = MR²/2
Solid sphere (centre): I = 2MR²/5
Hollow sphere (centre): I = 2MR²/3
Theorems: Parallel Axis: I = I_cm + Md²
Perpendicular Axis: I_z = I_x + I_y (plane laminas only)
NEET Tip
Only 6 to 7 standard shapes are repeatedly asked. Do not memorise exotic shapes — derive them using the Parallel or Perpendicular Axis Theorem from the basic ones. NEET rewards understanding over memorisation here.

2. Torque (τ)

Torque is the rotational equivalent of force — it causes angular acceleration. NEET tests both the definition (τ = r × F = rF sinθ) and the equation linking it to angular acceleration (τ = Iα). Equilibrium problems — where net torque is zero — appear frequently.

Key relations: τ = r × F = rF sinθ
τ = Iα
Equilibrium condition: Σ F = 0 AND Στ = 0 (both must hold)

3. Angular Momentum (L) and its Conservation

Angular Momentum L = Iω is the rotational equivalent of linear momentum. When no external torque acts on a system, L is conserved. NEET tests this through specific scenarios — recognise these instantly:

  • An ice skater pulling in her arms — I decreases, ω increases, L constant
  • A star collapsing under gravity — radius decreases, rotation speed increases
  • A rotating disc with a person walking from edge to centre
  • A bullet embedding in a rotating door
Watch for this in questions
The phrase "no external torque" in a question is the direct signal to apply conservation of angular momentum. If you see it, write L_initial = L_final immediately and solve from there.

4. Rolling Motion

Rolling without slipping means the contact point has zero velocity. NEET questions test the velocity of the centre of mass, the kinetic energy split between translation and rotation, and which shape reaches the bottom of an inclined plane first.

For pure rolling (no slip): v_cm = ωR
KE_total = ½mv² (1 + I/mR²)
Fraction of KE in rotation: KE_rot / KE_total = (I/mR²) / (1 + I/mR²)
NEET Classic: Inclined Plane Race
Which reaches the bottom first — a ring, disc, or solid sphere? The one with the smaller I/mR² ratio wins. Solid sphere (2/5) beats disc (1/2) beats ring (1). This comes up almost every two years in NEET.

Common mistakes that cost marks

✗ Wrong: Using I = MR² for all circular shapes
✓ Right: Ring = MR², Disc = MR²/2, Solid sphere = 2MR²/5

Always check: solid or hollow? Full shape or thin shell? The axis matters.

✗ Wrong: Applying Perpendicular Axis Theorem to 3D objects
✓ Right: Perpendicular Axis Theorem works only for plane laminas (flat 2D shapes)

For a sphere or cylinder, use Parallel Axis Theorem only.

✗ Wrong: Conserving angular momentum when friction acts externally
✓ Right: Check for external torque first — friction on a surface IS an external torque

Conservation of L only applies when the net external torque is zero.

✗ Wrong: Forgetting the rolling constraint v = ωR in rolling motion problems
✓ Right: Write v_cm = ωR as the first equation in every pure-rolling problem

This constraint links the two equations for translation and rotation.

2-Week revision plan for Rotational Motion

This plan is designed for a student who has already covered the chapter once and needs to consolidate for NEET. Adjust the pace to your current preparation level.

Day 1–2
Revise all standard Moment of Inertia formulas. Derive I for a disc and rod from scratch (don't look). Solve 15 NCERT + PYQ problems on MI and Radius of Gyration.
Day 3–4
Torque: equilibrium problems and τ = Iα applications. Solve 10 problems where you must find the pivot and calculate net torque. Common source: NEET 2018, 2020, 2022 papers.
Day 5
Angular Momentum conservation. Identify the "no external torque" trigger in 10 different problem types. Practice ice-skater, collapsing-star, and spinning-disc scenarios.
Day 6–7
Rolling Motion. Kinetic energy split, velocity of different points on a rolling body, inclined plane problems. Solve 15 problems including the classic "which reaches bottom first" type.
Day 8–9
Mixed PYQ practice: solve 30 NEET PYQs from 2016 to 2025 on this chapter under timed conditions (45 seconds per question). Note every mistake.
Day 10–11
Error analysis: revisit every mistake from Day 8–9. Identify whether it was a formula gap, conceptual gap, or calculation error. Fix each one specifically.
Day 12–14
Final revision: one formula sheet from memory, 10 quick problems, then leave the chapter. Revisiting endlessly hurts more than it helps — move to the next chapter once you can solve 8 of 10 PYQs correctly.

Frequently asked questions

How many questions come from Rotational Motion in NEET?

1 to 2 questions (4 to 8 marks) every year. Analysis of 2016–2025 NEET papers shows Moment of Inertia and Torque are the most consistently asked sub-topics.

Is Rotational Motion in NEET 2026 syllabus?

Yes, with updates. Basic Concept of Rotational Motion (ω, α) is newly added. Momentum Conservation, Centre of Mass Motion, and Centre of Mass of Uniform Rod have been removed. Check your material is aligned with the 2026 NTA syllabus.

What is the easiest way to remember Moment of Inertia formulas?

Group shapes by axis: central axis, diameter, and tangent. Learn the 6 to 7 standard shapes NEET repeatedly asks, then use the Parallel Axis Theorem (I = I_cm + Md²) for any shifted axis. Deriving the result is faster under exam pressure than trying to recall a memorised list.

What is the difference between Torque and Angular Momentum in NEET problems?

Torque (τ = Iα) causes a change in rotation — it is the rotational equivalent of force. Angular Momentum (L = Iω) describes the current state of rotation. When external torque is zero, L is conserved. Most NEET problems test one or the other — reading the question carefully reveals which.
